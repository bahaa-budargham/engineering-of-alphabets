Here is the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) representation for the 22 letters of the Modern Hebrew alphabet, along with their usual names:

א (Alef) – /ʔ/ (glottal stop, often silent in modern speech)

ב (Bet) – /b/ (with dagesh בּ) or /v/ (without dagesh ב)

ג (Gimel) – /ɡ/

ד (Dalet) – /d/

ה (He) – /h/ (often silent at the end of words)

ו (Vav) – /v/ (also used for long vowels /o/ and /u/)

ז (Zayin) – /z/

ח (Chet) – /χ/ (voiceless uvular fricative)

ט (Tet) – /t/

י (Yod) – /j/ (also used for long vowels /i/ and /e/)

כ / ך (Kaf / Final Kaf) – /k/ (with dagesh כּ) or /χ/ (without dagesh כ)

ל (Lamed) – /l/

מ / ם (Mem / Final Mem) – /m/

נ / ן (Nun / Final Nun) – /n/

ס (Samekh) – /s/

ע (Ayin) – /ʕ/ (historically) or /ʔ/ / silent (Modern Israeli)

פ / ף (Pe / Final Pe) – /p/ (with dagesh פּ) or /f/ (without dagesh פ)

צ / ץ (Tsadi / Final Tsadi) – /ts/

ק (Qof) – /k/

ר (Resh) – /ʁ/ (voiced uvular fricative in Modern Israeli)

ש (Shin / Sin) – /ʃ/ (Shin שׁ) or /s/ (Sin שׂ)

ת (Tav) – /t/
